About the Composite AQI (Air Quality Index)

The composite AQI is calculated by evaluating the individual concentrations of PM2.5, PM10, and NO2 using standard breakpoint tables. Each pollutant is assigned an individual index value, and the overall AQI is determined by the highest of these individual indices. This ensures that the final score reflects the most significant health risk present in the air.

About AQI & Guidelines

Understanding Air Quality Index and WHO Limits.

What is AQI?

The Air Quality Index (AQI) is a yardstick that runs from 0 to 500. The higher the AQI value, the greater the level of air pollution and the greater the health concern.

  • 0-50: Good - Air quality is satisfactory.
  • 51-100: Moderate - Acceptable, but may be a risk for some people.
  • 101-150: Unhealthy for Sensitive Groups - General public is less likely to be affected.
  • 151-200: Unhealthy - Everyone may begin to experience health effects.
  • 201-300: Very Unhealthy - Health alert: everyone may experience more serious health effects.
  • 301+: Hazardous - Health warnings of emergency conditions. The entire population is more likely to be affected.

WHO Air Quality Guidelines (2021)

The World Health Organization (WHO) has established strict guidelines to protect public health from the harmful effects of air pollution.

PM2.5 (Fine Particulate Matter)

Annual average: 5 µg/m³

24-hour average: 15 µg/m³

PM10 (Coarse Particulate Matter)

Annual average: 15 µg/m³

24-hour average: 45 µg/m³

NO2 (Nitrogen Dioxide)

Annual average: 10 µg/m³

24-hour average: 25 µg/m³

Sources: World Health Organization, OpenAQ, US EPA.
